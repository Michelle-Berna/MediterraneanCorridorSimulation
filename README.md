# Modeling and Evaluation of the Mediterranean Corridor

A **pre-feasibility study** of the Mediterranean Corridor (Barcelona–Valencia), focused on transportation demand modeling, railway workshop planning, dry port implementation, and social/private project assessment.

**Author:** Michelle Berna  
**Date:** October 2023  
**Institution:** Polytechnic University of Catalonia (UPC), Vilanova i la Geltrú  
**Keywords:** transportation demand modeling, railway workshop planning, dry port implementation, social and private evaluation, pre-feasibility study, Mediterranean Corridor.

---

## Context

The Mediterranean Corridor project aims to connect Barcelona and Valencia through a high-speed railway network, reducing travel times and improving the transport of people and goods. The study assesses the economic, social, and operational feasibility of this infrastructure as a basis for future project phases.

---

## Objectives

- Model passenger and freight transport demand.
- Plan railway workshops for preventive and corrective maintenance.
- Evaluate the implementation of dry ports as intermodal nodes.
- Conduct social and private assessment through cost-benefit analysis.
- Determine project viability as a pre-feasibility study.

---

## Methodology

### Transport demand
- Discrete choice models and random utility theory.
- Multinomial Logit (MNL) model.
- Revealed Preference (RP) and Stated Preference (SP) surveys.
- Pivot and Equi-Varied Pivot methods.
- k-means segmentation to disaggregate transport modes.
- Monte Carlo simulation to calibrate demand and fleet requirements.

### Railway workshops
- Poisson processes and M/M/1 model to estimate train arrivals.
- Track planning for preventive and corrective maintenance.

### Dry ports
- Optimization of the linear transport cost function.
- Origin-destination freight matrix.
- Evaluation of logistics cost savings.

### Assessment
- Social and private cost-benefit analysis.
- NPV and IRR calculation.

---

## Key Results

| Component | Main result |
|---|---|
| Barcelona–Tarragona demand | 62,844 individuals/day adjusted from 2017, with 5% annual demand growth and 30% induced demand; projected **103,373 passengers** |
| Required fleet | Mean of **39.92 trains**, approximately **40 trains** with 450 passengers capacity |
| Estimated modal shift | Rapid train: **16%**; bus: **19%**; train: **15%** |
| Railway workshops | **3 tracks** for preventive maintenance and **1 track** for corrective maintenance |
| Dry ports | Average savings of **18.21%** for Europe and **24.85%** for Spain |
| High-speed rail — social | IRR: **4%**; NPV: **USD 2.081 million** |
| High-speed rail — private | IRR: **19%**; NPV: **USD 1.470 million** |
| Dry port | IRR: **16%**; NPV: **USD 19.215 million** |

> Note: figures are reported as they appear in the document. Some IRR/NPV values should be checked for consistency before final publication.

---

## Conclusions

- The Mediterranean Corridor shows relevant social and private benefits.
- High-speed rail improves connectivity, reduces travel times, and contributes to lower congestion and emissions.
- Dry ports optimize freight logistics and reduce urban congestion.
- Railway workshops require operational planning based on demand and maintenance needs.
- Results support initiating a formal project phase: public participation, expropriations, detailed engineering, etc.
- Informed strategic decisions are recommended, such as prioritizing the Tarragona–Valencia arc.

---

## Recommendations

- Public-private collaboration.
- Long-term economic, social, and environmental impact studies.
- Financial diversification.
- Technological innovation.
- Community involvement.
- Continuous monitoring and evaluation.
- Sustainable focus.
- Development of specialized personnel.
- Adaptive flexibility.
- Effective communication.
